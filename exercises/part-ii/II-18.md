**Input:** `t1(trials, people, seed)` (1 row).

## Question

How often do two or more people in a group of 23 share a birthday?

## Solution

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE n(x) AS(
SELECT 1
UNION ALL
SELECT x+1
FROM n
WHERE x<(
SELECT trials
FROM t1)),p(k) AS(
SELECT 1
UNION ALL
SELECT k+1
FROM p
WHERE k<(
SELECT people
FROM t1)),g AS(
SELECT x,count(DISTINCT rnd((
SELECT seed
FROM t1)+x*1000+k)%365) distinct_days
FROM n
CROSS
JOIN p
GROUP BY x)
SELECT count(*),sum(distinct_days<(
SELECT people
FROM t1)),avg(distinct_days<(
SELECT people
FROM t1))
FROM g;
```

## Results

| --- | --- | --- |
| 10000 | 5081 | 0.5081 |

## Explanation

**Method and assumptions:** the summary counts how many of 10,000 groups of 23 share a birthday and reports the frequency. Birthdays are independent and uniformly distributed over 365 days, with no leap days or seasonal effects. The theoretical probability is about 0.5073.

**Edge cases and trade-offs:** with one person in each group, none of the 100 trials has a shared birthday.

UPDATE t1 SET people=1,trials=100;
