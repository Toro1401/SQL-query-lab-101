**Input:** `t1(parent_id, child_id, age, gender)` (8 rows).

## Solution

```sql
WITH a AS(
SELECT *,age-lag(age) OVER(PARTITION BY parent_id
ORDER BY age,child_id) gap
FROM t1)
SELECT parent_id,count(*),cast(avg(1.0*age) AS INTEGER),max(age)-min(age),coalesce(max(gap),0),min(age),max(age),group_concat(gender,', '
ORDER BY age,child_id)
FROM a
GROUP BY parent_id;
```

**Result and verification:** result 1: 4 rows. [[1001,2,10,4,4,8,12,"M, F"],[2002,3,10,7,5,7,14,"F, F, M"],[3003,2,13,2,2,12,14,"F, M"],[4004,1,7,0,0,7,7,"M"]]

## Explanation

**Method and assumptions:** each family gets a row with the difference between the oldest and youngest ages and the largest gap between consecutive ages. Genders follow age and ID order. The mean is truncated to match the source, and a single child gives zero age gaps.

**Edge cases and trade-offs:** for an only child aged 10, the mean is 10 and all gaps are zero.
